Gannon Hacks Cyber Club Adjudged Best New Club at Awards Ceremony

Gannon Hacks Cyber Club was named Best New Club at this year's Gannon Awards Ceremony, capping off a semester in which cybersecurity education at Gannon went well beyond the classroom. Gannon University cybersecurity students spent the year putting their skills to work in meaningful ways, through faculty mentorship, student-led innovation, and hands-on experience that rarely stayed confined to a lecture hall.

A Year Built on More Than Coursework

From launching the Gannon Hacks Cyber Club and its cybersecurity training portal, to presenting research, publishing at IEEE conferences, competing in Capture-the-Flag competitions, and attending national cybersecurity conferences, students gained experience that goes well past what a syllabus can cover. Each of those threads reinforced the others: research questions turned into workshop topics, workshop topics turned into CTF practice, and CTF practice turned into results on the competitive circuit.

On the Competitive Circuit

The club's Capture-the-Flag team placed fourth out of more than 20 teams at BSidesROC 2026 in Rochester, holding first place for much of the six-hour competition before a late scoring shift pushed the final standing down. It was the kind of result that comes from the weekly technical training sessions the club runs every Wednesday, not from a single semester of cramming.

On the Road

Beyond competition, members represented the club at OWASP AppSec Boston, where they connected with security professionals across the public and private sectors and picked up an invitation to return for the Austin, Texas edition this October, and at the HIMSS Virginia Chapter's Cybersecurity & AI Summit in Charlottesville, where they sat in on sessions covering adversarial AI, AI governance, and a healthcare ransomware tabletop exercise. Closer to home, the club also opened its doors to local high school students for a hands-on introduction to the field, extending the same hands-on approach that defines its own training sessions to the next generation of prospective cybersecurity students.

What the Recognition Means

Best New Club is a recognition of all of it at once: the weekly training sessions, the research, the competition results, and the willingness of members to travel for conferences on their own time. It belongs to everyone who showed up to a Wednesday session, ran a workshop, or represented the club on the road this year.

Best New Club is a great start. Here's to building on it next year, more research, more competitions, and more conferences.
